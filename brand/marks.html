Boat marks · v2

One shape, cut once, still readable from the far side of the mooring.

A 3-metre boat gets one mark, not an identity system. Everything here is built backwards from a single sheet of vinyl and a 60 mm minimum.

What changed

The honeycomb went overboard

The first round built the mark around a hexagon — a comb cell, a honey story. That was an identity for a food brand. A dinghy is a different problem: the mark gets cut from one sheet of vinyl, stuck on a curved hull at maybe 100 mm across, and has to survive sun, salt and a scrub brush. So the abstract route won, because it is the only one that holds up under those rules.

Two swept wings, a head, three tapering bands. It reads twice — a bee seen from above, and the wake of something small moving fast.

No holes
Every piece is positive. Nothing has to be weeded out of an interior, so nothing floats off the backing sheet on the way to the hull.
No hairlines
The thinnest element is a wing tip at 2.8 mm when the mark is cut 60 mm tall. That is the floor, and it sets the minimum size.
No sharp points
Every corner is rounded. Sharp tips lift first — they catch a sponge, a fender, a bare foot.

Colour

Three, and the hull picks

Two-colour is a layered cut — it costs a second sheet and a registration step. It looks better; one colour is what you order if you want it done this week.

Navy#12283F On white, grey or wood hulls. The default.
Honey#E8A33D The wings in two-colour. Alone only on navy.
Foam#F7F4EC On navy, black or dark grey hulls and tubes.
