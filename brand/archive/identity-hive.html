The idea

The bee gets a cell of its own

A single bee is charming but generic — every honey brand has one. The hexagon is what makes it specific: it turns the mark into one cell of a comb, which means it tiles. Set the mark beside itself and it builds a hive; use one cell as a container for anything — a photo, a number, a person’s face — and the identity keeps extending without ever redrawing the bee.

Three directions

Same bee, three temperaments

All three are drawn from one geometry, so you can change your mind later without redrawing anything.

Recommended

01 — Hive Cell

Solid, confident, unmistakable at 16 px. The container does the heavy lifting: it holds shape as an app icon, a stamp, a pin, a favicon. Warmest and most commercial of the three.

Alternate

02 — Flight Line

One line weight, one dotted trail. Editorial and hand-made — right for packaging, letterpress, embroidery, a menu header. Needs room; it dies below about 40 px.

Alternate

03 — Stripe

Abstract to the last possible step: two wings, three tapering bands. The most modern and the most scalable — it survives being stitched, embossed or rendered a centimetre wide.

Palette

Honey, not highlighter

The trap with a bee is cadmium yellow. Everything here is pulled a half-step towards amber and brown, so it reads as food and craft rather than warning tape.

Usage

Four ways to keep it intact

✓ Do

Scale the whole mark proportionally. The cell and the bee are one object.

✕ Don’t

Stretch the cell to fill a space. Use the stacked lockup instead.

✕ Don’t

Rotate the cell. It sits point-up, always — that is what makes it tile.

✕ Don’t

Place the honey cell on a mid-tone colour. Ink, wax or a photo’s dark area only.
